Three users in a single week tried to click on a thing in my product
that was not a button. It looked like text. It was text. But they had all
read it as "click me." I had not seen it for two years and they saw it
the first time.

## What I had built

A label, set in bold, that summarised the state of the page. A counter,
basically. Twelve points to bold text, sat next to a sleeker secondary
button. Visually, the bold text was the heaviest thing on the screen.
Weight reads as importance. Importance reads as actionable.

The three users were not wrong. I had built a button by accident.

## What I changed

Two options. I could make the text less prominent, or I could make it a
real button. I made it a real button. The button now opens a panel with
the underlying detail. The panel is useful. The thing the users were
trying to reach turned out to exist, but I had been hiding it behind a
"more info" link three pixels tall.

## What I learned

Visual weight is a promise. If your strongest visual element does not
respond to a click, you are breaking a promise. Either the element
shouldn't be the strongest, or the click shouldn't be unanswered. Most
of the time, the click should be answered.
