In early March I removed the timeline app from my home screen, on three
devices, for a full week. No replacement. No filtered feed. No daily
digest.

## What I did instead

I read longer pieces, not better ones. I shipped two small things that
had been sitting in draft. I wrote one of these posts. I went to bed
forty minutes earlier on average — not because I planned to, but because
I had nothing to scroll into the night with.

I also got bored, which is the part I had forgotten about.

## What I missed

A few specific things, all of which someone DM'd me about within
forty-eight hours. One funding round in a company I care about. One
release note from a tool I use. The cultural moments — the meme, the
discourse — passed me by entirely, and I noticed they had passed me by
without being able to tell what they had been.

The cost of missing them was zero. The benefit of catching them in real
time, when I had been catching them, was also approximately zero. They
were just texture.

## What I am doing now

I check the timeline twice on weekdays. Morning coffee and right before
dinner. Not as a discipline, more as a default. The default holds without
effort because I no longer expect anything specific to be there.
